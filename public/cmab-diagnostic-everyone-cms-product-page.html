What It Means

1. You asked the model a question it had no way to answer

A contextual bandit learns by watching which version earns more of one chosen goal, then sending more of the right visitors to the right version. The chosen goal here recorded zero events in 79,128 decisions. With no wins and no losses to compare, there is no basis to prefer any version over any other — so splitting traffic evenly is the mathematically correct output, not a bug. This test is not broken. It was asked an unanswerable question for 55 weeks.

2. The goal isn't tracked on this page

The holdback group sees the original, unmodified page. If the goal were real and simply suppressed by your variations, the holdback would still record it. It recorded 0 / 4,029. On the sister bandit's page the identical goal records 18 / 2,620 (0.687%) in holdback. Conclusion: the event exists in your project but is not firing on the CMS product page. Fix the tracking or pick a goal that already works here.

3. The healthy-looking numbers belong to goals the model ignored

Form success shows thousands of conversions and even a “significant” flag. None of that influenced allocation. Only the primary goal trains the bandit; Form success and Navigation CTA are observational passengers. Do not read the Form success result as evidence the model had something to learn from — it did not.

For the record, as an observation only: on the campaign rollup (campaign.metrics[1]) personalised visitors converted on Form success at 1.169% versus holdback 1.440% — personalisation was 18.8% worse, and that comparison is not flagged conclusive. Which is exactly what you'd expect from a model that never learned anything: you paid the cost of showing four rotating messages and got no targeting benefit back.

4. Nobody can tell you what this model learned — including the model

The attribution payload is empty. There is no ranking of which visitor characteristics mattered, and no per-version breakdown of who responded well or badly. That is consistent with a model that never trained. Separately, 13 of the 17 visitor characteristics are unlabelled ID numbers like 30266011077 — so even once attribution does appear, most of it will be unreadable to a marketer without a lookup table.

5. The results you're looking at are four months stale

The experience says “Running” but traffic stopped on 17 June 2026 — 108 days of zeros. Anyone opening this test today sees a live-looking test that has in fact been dormant since June. Treat every number here as a snapshot of a finished period, not a current reading.

The diagnostic insight: it's a counting difference, not a population difference

Across every metric the denominators agree — 75,099 personalised decisions in both the campaign rollup and the experience rollup, and 79,128 vs 79,122 total (a 6-decision gap, 0.008%, immaterial). Only the numerators diverge, by a consistent 2.69× on Form success and 1.82× on Navigation CTA.

Because the two pipelines are looking at the same people, the gap is in how a conversion is counted, not who was counted:

  • Decision-scoped counts every page decision as a separate opportunity — one visitor who converts across three sessions counts three times.
  • Session-unique collapses each session to at most one conversion — the same visitor counts once per session.

This metric is configured as unique / session (experiment.metrics[].aggregator = "unique", .scope = "session"). The results-page figure (878) honours that setting. The experience-level figure (2,359) does not appear to. The configured setting is the one that should win — which makes the lower number the defensible one and the higher one the anomaly. We are flagging, not resolving, this: confirming which pipeline is authoritative needs Optimizely engineering.
