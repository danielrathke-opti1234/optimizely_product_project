Experiment Test Plan

Problem-First Hero Headline vs. Aspirational Control

Determine whether replacing the aspirational homepage hero headline with a problem-focused alternative increases scroll engagement and demo sign-up rate — isolating the copy effect cleanly from any CTA changes.

A/B Test Homepage Hero Awareness → Demo Request
Experiment Summary
Primary Decision
Adopt problem-first copy as the homepage hero default
Audience
All homepage visitors — no audience condition
Devices
All devices — desktop, tablet, and mobile
Traffic
100% eligible homepage traffic, split 50/50
Opportunity
The Optimizely homepage hero is the first impression for every visitor. The current headline, “You’re free to grow,” leads with brand aspiration — a message that resonates broadly but does not immediately signal relevance to in-market B2B buyers who are already feeling the pain of fragmented martech, wasted spend, and difficulty proving impact. These buyers need to see their problem named before they invest attention. A problem-first headline creates that instant recognition and increases the likelihood that qualified visitors scroll deeper and pursue a demo rather than bouncing off an abstract promise.
Supporting Rationale
A prior test on this same homepage — the “B2B Tech: Demo Request Funnel” experiment — showed that the “Industry Trend Thought Leadership” variation, which led with the same problem-agitation framing (“Is Your Marketing Getting Lost in the Noise?”), delivered +37.7% lift in product-landing-page visits over the aspirational control (statistically significant). The “Workshop Offer” variation drove +34.2% (significant). Both beat the aspirational control convincingly, with downstream Demo Request Started and Complete metrics trending positive. Sample sizes in that test were modest at 256–299 visitors per variation, and the prior winning variation also changed the CTA — so this test is designed to produce a cleaner, better-powered answer by isolating the headline and subheadline copy effect specifically.
Hypothesis
If we replace the hero headline with “Is your marketing getting lost in the noise?” and update the subheadline to focus on turning anonymous visitors into loyal customers, then more in-market visitors will scroll deeper and initiate a demo request, because problem-agitation framing creates immediate relevance for B2B buyers experiencing marketing complexity — motivating them to explore rather than bounce.
Change
Hero headline and subheadline copy only. CTA text, button style, hero background image, text placement, and font styling are unchanged.
Expected Behavior
Higher scroll depth past 50%, more clicks into product pages, and an increase in Demo Request Started events among homepage visitors.
Business Effect
More qualified visitors enter the demo funnel, increasing demo sign-up volume and accelerating pipeline generation.
Experience Design
Control — Aspirational Hero
Current live experience — no changes
50% traffic

No changes. Headline: “You’re free to grow.” Subheadline: “The AI platform to create and optimize every digital experience. So the experience-makers can do the work they love.” CTA: “Let’s grow.”
Rationale
Serves as the baseline against which all variation metrics are measured. Captures the brand-aspirational positioning currently in market.
Variation 1 — Problem-First Hero
Headline and subheadline copy swapped; all other elements identical
50% traffic
Key Changes
Headline replaced with: “Is your marketing getting lost in the noise?” Subheadline replaced with: “The AI platform to create, test, and optimize every digital experience — so you can turn anonymous visitors into loyal customers.” Hero background, text placement, font styling, and CTA (“Let’s grow”) are unchanged.
Rationale
Problem-agitation framing creates immediate recognition for in-market B2B buyers. The subheadline pairs the platform capability statement with a concrete customer outcome, sharpening relevance without abandoning product positioning. Keeping all other hero elements constant ensures the result is attributable to copy alone.
Audience and Targeting
Activation

Audience
All visitors — no audience condition applied. Segmentation analysis by new vs. returning and channel is recommended post-hoc.
Devices
All devices — desktop, tablet, and mobile. Confirm hero rendering and text truncation on mobile viewports before launch.
Allocation
100% of eligible homepage traffic. Equal 50/50 split between Control and Variation 1.
Measurement Plan
Role Metric Type Direction Why It Matters
Primary Demo Request Started Conversion Rate ↑ Increase Directly measures in-funnel pull generated by the new headline — the closest observable proxy to demo intent on the page. Aligns with the business objective of increasing demo sign-ups.
Diagnostic Scroll Depth ≥50% Conversion Rate ↑ Increase Leading indicator of whether problem framing captures attention and motivates exploration. Expected to move before downstream conversions, helping interpret causality if Demo Request Started is slow to accumulate.
Diagnostic Visit: Product Landing Page Conversion Rate ↑ Increase Replicates the primary metric from the prior B2B Tech test, enabling direct benchmarking against the +37.7% prior result and confirming whether the headline effect on page exploration holds when the CTA is held constant.
Guardrail Demo Request Complete Conversion Rate Monitor Confirms that any increase in started requests carries through to form submission. A drop relative to Demo Request Started signals friction in the funnel below the hero, not a headline problem.
Guardrail Bounce / Single-Page Sessions Session Rate ↓ Decrease Ensures the problem headline does not alienate brand-awareness visitors or cause an increase in immediate exits. A meaningful rise here would indicate the copy is narrowing appeal in a way that harms top-of-funnel volume.
Confirm that the selected metrics are available and correctly scoped before launch.
Decision Criteria
✅ Adopt
Variation 1 reaches a reliable improvement in Demo Request Started with scroll depth and product-page visits trending positively. No guardrail degradation. Optimizely signals a confident decision. Ship the problem-first copy as the new homepage hero default.
🔄 Iterate
Demo Request Started moves directionally but does not reach the decision threshold, or scroll depth improves without downstream conversion movement. Run a follow-on test pairing this headline with a sharpened CTA or a refined subheadline to continue building on the signal.
❌ Do Not Adopt
Variation 1 shows no meaningful lift in Demo Request Started, scroll depth is flat or negative, and guardrails are neutral or negative. Retain the aspirational headline and consider alternative interventions — such as a mid-page or form-adjacent copy test — to improve funnel entry.
🔍 Investigate
Demo Request Complete drops despite Demo Request Started improving (indicating funnel breakage below the hero), results diverge sharply by device type, or a Sample Ratio Mismatch is detected. Pause, audit event scoping and variation rendering, and resolve before drawing conclusions.
Risks and QA
Interpretation Risk
Limited Power on Downstream Metrics
The prior test ran at 256–299 visitors per variation — sufficient for page-visit metrics but insufficient to confirm downstream conversion effects. This test must run long enough to accumulate meaningful sample on Demo Request Started. Do not stop on an early scroll-depth signal alone; Optimizely's decision engine should reach confidence on the primary metric before the experiment is called.
Design Consideration
Clean CTA Isolation
The prior winning variation changed both the headline and the CTA; this test holds the CTA constant. If results here are weaker than the +37.7% benchmark, note in reporting that the prior CTA change may have contributed to that lift — and plan a follow-on test to evaluate the CTA effect separately if this variation wins.
QA Check
Variation Rendering
Confirm the headline and subheadline swap is correct in Variation 1 across desktop, tablet, and mobile breakpoints. Verify that the CTA text, button styling, hero background image, and text placement are pixel-identical to the Control before activating traffic.
QA Check
Event Scoping and Firing
Validate that Demo Request Started, Demo Request Complete, scroll-depth, and Visit: Product Landing Page events are correctly scoped to the homepage and firing as expected in both Control and Variation 1 before launch.
QA Check
Experiment Overlap
Confirm that no concurrent experiments are targeting the homepage hero zone. Overlapping tests on the same component can contaminate the traffic split, inflate or deflate observed effects, and make results uninterpretable.
Recommended Run Approach
Run through at least two complete business cycles — typically four weeks for a B2B SaaS homepage — and continue until Optimizely reaches a reliable decision on Demo Request Started. Because the demo form sits below the fold on a long page, Demo Request Started and Complete events accumulate more slowly than scroll-depth events; allow sufficient time for these to reach confidence before calling the experiment. Avoid stopping early based on directional scroll-depth or product-page-visit movement alone. Post-hoc segment analysis by acquisition channel (paid vs. organic vs. direct) and new vs. returning visitors is recommended to understand where the headline effect is strongest.
